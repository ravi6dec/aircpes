About the conference

Energy systems are becoming cyber-physical systems

Rising electricity demand, decarbonisation targets, the transition of conventional consumers to active prosumers, and the requirement of highly secure communication interfaces are pushing conventional, infrastructure-centric power grids toward complex, bidirectional, data-rich cyber-physical systems — closely integrating generation, transmission, distribution, and storage assets with sensing, communication, computing, and control layers.

Modern energy infrastructure is fundamentally a cyber-physical system, in which physical assets such as generation units, transmission and distribution networks, microgrids, batteries, electric vehicles, and smart appliances are closely integrated with digital layers. This integration creates significant opportunities for artificial intelligence while simultaneously introducing new vulnerabilities. AIR-CPES 2027 provides a platform for contributions from both the electrical engineering and computer science communities, encouraging interdisciplinary research connecting power systems, AI, communications, computing, cybersecurity, and data science.

Together, the two volumes aim to provide an interdisciplinary reference for researchers, academicians, engineers, industry professionals, policymakers, and practitioners working at the intersection of AI, energy systems, cybersecurity, digitalisation, and resilience.

VOLUME I

AI for Smart Grids, Microgrids & Distributed Energy

Data-driven forecasting and optimisation, AI-based monitoring, control, protection, fault diagnosis, digital twins, predictive maintenance, and AI-enabled cyber-threat and anomaly detection for resilient, self-healing systems.

VOLUME II

Energy Storage, Electric Mobility & Smart Energy Services

AI-enabled battery management, state-of-health estimation, second-life battery assessment, smart charging, vehicle-to-grid technologies, intelligent energy markets, smart buildings and cities, and decentralised rural energy systems.

Thematic tracks

Eight tracks

TRACK 01

Artificial Intelligence and Data-Driven Energy Systems

Artificial intelligence, machine learning, deep learning, reinforcement learning, generative AI, computational intelligence, big data analytics, data-driven modelling, optimisation, forecasting, decision-making and intelligent control for modern energy systems.

More scope

Including explainable and trustworthy AI, federated and privacy-preserving learning, physics-informed machine learning, transfer learning, edge AI/TinyML, and open datasets and benchmarks for energy AI.

TRACK 02

Cyber-Physical Energy Systems, Smart Grids and Microgrids

Cyber-physical energy systems, smart grids, intelligent microgrids, distributed energy resources, distributed generation, energy management, demand response, virtual power plants, real-time monitoring and control, grid automation, system stability and intelligent coordination of interconnected energy assets.

More scope

Including AI for power system state estimation, power flow, unit commitment, congestion management and transmission/distribution planning; AI-based control of grid-forming and grid-following inverters, low-inertia grids, HVDC/FACTS and power quality; and AI for island, remote, off-grid and disaster-relief microgrids.

TRACK 03

Resilience, Reliability and Cybersecurity

Energy system resilience, reliability, cyber-physical security, cyber-threat detection, intrusion detection, anomaly detection, cybersecurity risk assessment, fault detection and diagnosis, self-healing systems, intelligent protection and control, disaster-resilient infrastructure and secure-by-design energy systems.

More scope

Including adversarial robustness of AI models, secure AI-enabled communication (5G/6G, IoT, satellite networks) and AI for extreme-weather and climate-driven outage prediction.

TRACK 04

Renewable and Sustainable Energy Systems

AI for renewable energy integration, solar photovoltaic systems, agrivoltaics, wind and hybrid renewable energy systems, renewable energy forecasting, resource assessment, energy efficiency, sustainable energy planning, decentralised and rural energy systems, energy access and climate-resilient energy infrastructure.

More scope

Including AI for hydro, geothermal, biomass, waste-to-energy and ocean energy; AI for green hydrogen, electrolysers, fuel cells and power-to-X; AI for carbon accounting, emissions tracking, CCUS and life-cycle assessment; and the AI-driven energy-water-food nexus and energy harvesting.

TRACK 05

Energy Storage, Batteries and Electric Mobility

Battery energy storage systems, battery management systems, state-of-charge and state-of-health estimation, battery degradation and remaining useful life prediction, second-life batteries, battery health grading and assessment, electric vehicles, smart EV charging, battery swapping, V2G, V2B and intelligent coordination of storage and electric mobility.

More scope

Including thermal and hydrogen storage, AI-based charging infrastructure planning, wireless EV charging, and electrification of heavy-duty, rail, maritime and aviation transport.

TRACK 06

Digital Twins, Predictive Maintenance and Intelligent Energy Infrastructure

Digital twins for energy systems, AI-enabled predictive maintenance, condition monitoring, asset management, intelligent diagnostics, IoT-enabled monitoring, intelligent sensors, edge and cloud computing, system modelling and simulation, virtual commissioning and AI-based operational decision support.

More scope

Including AI for thermal systems (district heating and cooling, heat pumps, CHP), industrial energy systems and process-industry efficiency, and interoperability standards (IEC 61850, OpenADR, IEEE 2030) for AI-enabled infrastructure.

TRACK 07

Intelligent Energy Markets, Smart Buildings and Smart Cities

AI applications in energy markets, electricity pricing and trading, peer-to-peer energy trading, blockchain-enabled energy transactions, prosumers and energy communities, demand-side flexibility, smart homes and buildings, building energy management, smart cities, integrated energy systems, intelligent mobility and data-driven energy services.

More scope

Including smart metering and AMI analytics, non-intrusive load monitoring (NILM), consumer load profiling, multi-energy and sector-coupled systems, AI for market design and regulation, and consumer behaviour, social acceptance and energy justice.

TRACK 08

Emerging Technologies, Applications and Case Studies

Emerging AI technologies and applications in energy systems, generative AI and large language models for energy, AI for climate adaptation and resilience, industry and utility applications, AI data centres integration, pilot and demonstration projects, community and rural energy applications, smart city deployments and interdisciplinary case studies.

More scope

Including field demonstrations, lessons learned and future directions; agentic AI, foundation models for energy, quantum and neuromorphic computing for energy applications, energy-efficient and sustainable AI, and AI governance, ethics and regulation in the energy sector.

Submission & review

Peer review & publishing policy

Every paper is sent to at least three reviewers; acceptance requires recommendations from at least two, with the final decision resting with the conference chair. All submissions are screened for plagiarism via Turnitin, with a required similarity index below 15%.

Review is double-blind — author identities are withheld from reviewers, and the author list must not appear in the submitted manuscript. Papers that do not conform to the double-blind policy are rejected without review.

How to submit: papers (max. 8 pages, PDF) are submitted through the EasyChair conference management system. The submission link will be published here — for queries, write to the conveners.
  • Only original, unpublished work will be considered
  • Submission via the EasyChair conference management system, in PDF format
  • No page numbers or special headers/footers in the manuscript
  • Changes to paper metadata permitted before the submission deadline
  • Self-plagiarism and dual submissions are treated as professional misconduct
  • A maximum of 10 papers assigned to any single reviewer
